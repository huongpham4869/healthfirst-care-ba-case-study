# HealthFirst Care Overview

*BA Case Study - Enhancing Operational Efficiency in a Multi-Specialty Hospital*

[← Project Summary](00-project-summary.md) · [README](../README.md) · [BRD →](02-brd.md)

## 1. Project Overview

This case study examines how a multi-specialty hospital could improve appointment management, operational resource visibility, patient communication, and coordination across departments.

The case materials highlight long waiting times and difficulty using the online booking process. The current-state process review also identifies limited visibility of operational resources and manual hand-offs between departments. In the available 20-record appointment sample, 12 appointments (60%) did not go ahead as originally scheduled: 8 (40%) were recorded as No Show or Cancelled, and 4 (20%) were rescheduled.

The 60% figure is a descriptive result for this sample, not a hospital-wide estimate or proof of root cause. The data does not record cancellation reasons or whether cancelled slots were reused, so it does not establish a lost-capacity rate. The analysis uses the case materials, stakeholder profiles, process review, and available data to define business needs, requirements, and future-state processes. System selection, development, and implementation are outside the case study scope.

## 2. Analysis Approach

The analysis follows a traceable line of reasoning: business problem → evidence → current state → root-cause analysis → business needs → requirements → future state → validation approach. The work was iterative. The BRD was refined as stakeholder, process, and data analysis progressed. Each requirement should be traceable to its business need, supporting evidence, priority, and verification approach in the RTM.

## 3. Scope

The project defines business needs and future-state process requirements; it does not commit to a specific technology or claim that the proposed changes have been implemented or measured in production.

**In Scope**

- Stakeholder analysis and a proposed engagement plan
- As-Is process analysis and root-cause analysis
- Data analysis
- Business, functional and non-functional requirements
- To-Be process models and gap analysis
- Requirements prioritisation and traceability
- Proposed acceptance/validation approach, assumptions, and limitations

**Out Of Scope**

- Vendor or product selection
- Technical architecture and detailed solution design
- System development, integration, implementation, deployment, and UAT execution
- Clinical diagnosis or treatment
- Recruitment, staffing-model, or workforce decisions
